# Indexed Showdown candidate scoring

The post-#196 profile still showed repeated scalar candidate roster scoring: 150,000 calls for 300 candidates and 500 scenarios. Instrumented profile time is not an unprofiled latency estimate. The prior real-input workload evaluated a 1,200-candidate validation/audit shortlist and spent roughly 27 seconds in candidate processing before this change.

This incremental change over #196 690c5e1 factors its structural roster indexing into an ordered shared helper and lazily indexes candidate rosters on their first evaluated frame. Each frame reads fresh outcome values once per player and computes Captain multiplication plus the same built-in FLEX sum in original slot order. Candidate order and duplicate occurrences remain intact. Per-candidate percentile, hit, payout, value and distribution calculations stay in the existing loop. No sampled values are cached in layouts. Each simulation call starts new layouts; pre-draw cancellation skips candidate indexing and warm replay still evaluates candidates against replayed fresh-frame values.

Three alternating paired synthetic fresh simulations against #196 used 1,000 candidates, 2,000 scenarios and a 4,000-entry field. Median simulation time fell from 11.067s to 9.801s (11.4%); nested candidate processing from 4.157s to 2.948s (29.1%). All six runs matched exact metrics, scenario hits/values and non-timing reports and completed 2,000 fresh scenarios with zero replay. Opponent construction and outcome sampling were unchanged. Timings include opponent generation, exclude candidate generation, and do not establish a full-build or real-input speedup. Nested phases are included in simulation time, not additive stages.

Three new regressions cover ordered/repeated/empty/single roster arithmetic with changed frame values, strict missing IDs, and full naive/indexed simulation parity including duplicate candidate order, metrics, scenario values, RNG states and distributions. Existing tests exercise cold-to-warm replay, payout reranking, partial cancellation, transformed outcomes and pre-compilation historical qualification.
