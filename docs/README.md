The Showdown **Earlier repeat finishes** and **Across more games** portfolio reviews also report WR/TE Captain with own QB and zero/one/two/three-or-more K/DST usage. Qualification uses strictly earlier calendar dates, at least three ranked salary slates and two earlier top-cutoff finishes, with the existing exact-entry-count and preset matching. All later entries are retained, including losses. A single unresolved roster withholds the entire portfolio's new usage rates. Receiver percentages use WR/TE Captain entries only; K/DST includes Captain and FLEX. Peer support is metric-specific, including the separate five/ten-peer checks. These are descriptive usage differences, not evidence of predictive skill, profit or a grouping-rule benefit. Source, cancellation, batching and consistency safeguards remain unchanged; no model or generation defaults change.

The earlier-qualified pairing metrics also appear in the cross-game checks. Each metric requires ten distinct supported cohort users per salary slate and at least five independent slates. Five- and ten-peer checks report game directions and leave-one-game-out ranges separately. A consistent direction describes usage, not improved outcomes; a label that changes with peer support is sensitive. Small games are withheld without broadening peers. Older reports without pairing evidence retain their original metrics.


Earlier-qualified Showdown pairing reviews now include **within-user later finish rates**. Both constructions must appear in the same user's later contest portfolio with the same exact Captain ID and salary band, meeting separate five/ten ranked-entry thresholds on each side. Rank cutoffs use the contest field-size/accepted-entry denominator, not that user's entry count. Every roster and rank in the user's portfolio must be resolved; otherwise its new outcome comparisons are withheld. Rank-one ties all count. Contrasts use overlap weights within users, equal users within contests, equal contests within salary slates and equal slates. The output reports supported users/slates, directions and leave-one-game-out ranges. These comparisons do not hold other construction choices fixed or establish causal effects, independence or profitable rules. Sparse/older evidence stays unavailable. No generation, forecast, SIM, ownership or grouping defaults change.


The within-user finish review also reports a separate contrast holding all four common grouping flags fixed: two kickers, two defenses, two QBs and QB with opposing DST. The original Captain/salary comparison stays separate. Both sides need five/ten ranked entries within the SAME fixed-flag stratum; smaller strata never pool to reach support. All incomplete-portfolio, earlier-cohort, source and cancellation safeguards remain. Combined primary/conditional cell storage uses the existing bound. Earlier reports without this evidence show it as unavailable. With all flags fixed, two total K/DST versus zero necessarily means one kicker plus one defense versus zero; double kickers/defenses cannot share the zero baseline's flags. Three-plus versus zero is structurally unavailable. These narrower comparisons still do not control individual FLEX identities or establish causal rule benefits. No defaults change.

The earlier-qualified outcome review includes a separate **Exactly one QB** receiver comparison: WR/TE Captain with its own-team QB versus the opposing QB. Both sides must contain exactly one QB and match the same user, contest, exact Captain ID, salary band and all four common grouping flags. Zero-QB and multiple-QB lineups cannot supply support; five/ten entries per side remain separate. Older evidence stays unavailable. This avoids mixing QB presence with QB-team pairing, but does not control individual QB skill, every FLEX choice or prove a causal stacking benefit. Primary and fixed-group results remain separate and unchanged. All three cell collections share the existing memory bound. No defaults or generation/model behavior change.

The within-user outcome report now displays both its original totals and a separate stricter game-support view: ten distinct supported cohort users per salary slate and at least five independent slates. Users repeated across contests in the same game count once toward support. Support remains specific to each metric, entry threshold, format and entry preset. Small games are withheld; fewer than five supported games are labeled insufficient independent games. Missing user identity cannot supply strict support. A supported descriptive sample is not a significance test or an automatic strategy recommendation. Original aggregate calculations remain unchanged; this view can render previously verified saved evidence without importing or rebuilding history.

Earlier-qualified pairing reports now start with **Pairing evidence at a glance**, before the full methods and calculations. It uses the existing stricter ten-entry-per-side, ten-distinct-user-per-game check. Fewer than five supported games show insufficient independent games without highlighting their mean in the summary. Missing legacy evidence and structurally unavailable comparisons stay distinct. Supported samples show descriptive top-cutoff differences, directions and leave-one-game-out ranges. All detailed five/ten-entry results remain below; no calculation, rule or model default changes.

Historical Portfolios offers **Full review overview** to return from an entrant or contest to the entire captured review without rescanning history. It preserves the username/contest search and remains available for the last completed result while another review runs.

Historical Portfolios offers **Save completed review** after a successful review. It saves a UTF-8 text snapshot with the full captured overview, captured source hash receipts and the selected view when different from the overview. It does not export every entrant detail, recheck current sources, import data or change rules/models. The saved capture is frozen before the native Save dialog opens; a newer background completion cannot replace it. During another review, the last completed capture can still be saved; pending/canceled work is never exported. The default destination uses the existing external review-reports folder, with an ordinary Save dialog to choose elsewhere. Atomic writes preserve existing files on failure; direct-write fallback is disabled. Only regular .txt files outside history and the app checkout are accepted; oversized exports fail without truncation. Cancel/failure leaves the completed review available for retry.


Historical Portfolios also offers **Single Entry Showdown - construction and later finishes**. Choose the history start, evaluation start and through date, then run the review. It uses only explicitly classified Single Entry Showdown contests and requires one indexed entry per verified username, including in earlier history. Earlier-selected users need at least three earlier ranked salary slates and two earlier top-cutoff finishes; same-day/future results do not qualify and later losses remain included.

The captured report audits the field against exact historical salaries and displays Captain position, WR/TE Captain with own QB, Captain/opposing DST, K/DST counts, majority-team splits, salary bands and common grouping flags. It separates earlier-cohort usage versus noncohort usage from field feature-present versus feature-absent finish rates (top 1%, top 0.1%, rank one, including ties). Raw denominators and excluded roster/rank counts are visible. Field-size scopes stay separate: under 1,000; 1,000-9,999; 10,000 or more; unknown. Missing field sizes use observed entries for rank cutoffs, without claiming field completeness.

Five- and ten-entry support checks are metric-specific. Contests average within salary game/date, then games equally; five supported games is only a descriptive minimum. Means are withheld from the summary for thinner samples. The report does not control exact Captain, salary or player quality in its raw associations. Entry-fee matching, payouts and ROI are unavailable. It changes no grouping rules, models or lineup selection. Existing source/index verification, cancellation and bounds apply; the new field scan is limited to one million entries per review and aborts rather than publishing a truncated audit. Use Save completed review for the captured text and source receipts.


Single Entry reports also distinguish **10,000 or more (observed lower bound; total unknown)** when the total field size is missing but matching captured contest/audit receipts contain at least 10,000 accepted entries. This scope stays separate from supplied field sizes. It does not change rank denominators, completeness, cohort eligibility or outcomes. Fewer observed entries cannot establish a small or medium field; missing/inconsistent older receipts remain unknown.


Single Entry reports now capture exact observed lineup duplication during the same source-verified field audit. Identity is Captain slot ID plus five unordered FLEX slot IDs. Per contest, report distinct observed rosters, observed singletons, shared rosters and entries, extra copies, maximum copy count, the copy distribution, and resolved rank-one entries versus distinct rank-one rosters. Different lineups tied on points are not treated as identical. All resolved rosters contribute, including missing-rank entries; finishing comparisons still require ranks. Unresolved or unseen entries can hide further copies, so observed singletons do not prove full-field uniqueness and no payout split is inferred. The existing 200,000-signature bound aborts without publishing a truncated audit. Older captures explicitly lack this evidence and require a fresh verified review; counts are never reconstructed from ranks or aggregate usage.
